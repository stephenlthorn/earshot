# Code of conduct

Earshot follows the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/). The full text is on that page, and it applies to everyone who takes part: in issues, pull requests, discussions and any other project space, and when you represent the project elsewhere.

## In short

We want this to be a welcoming place for everyone, whatever their background, identity or experience.

What we expect:

- Be respectful and kind, including when you disagree.
- Give and accept feedback about the work, not the person.
- Own your mistakes, apologise to the people they affected, and learn from them.
- Think about what is best for the community as a whole.

What isn't acceptable:

- sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention;
- trolling, insults and personal or political attacks;
- harassment, in public or in private;
- publishing someone's private information, such as an address or an email, without their permission;
- anything else that would reasonably be seen as inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour worries you, tell the maintainer privately. Open a private report with GitHub's [Report a vulnerability](https://github.com/stephenlthorn/earshot/security/advisories/new) form, which only the maintainer can see. Start the title with "Conduct:" so it isn't mistaken for a security report.

Every report is reviewed and kept confidential. Say what happened, where, and when, with links if you have them.

## Enforcement

The maintainer decides what happens after a report, following the Contributor Covenant's enforcement guidelines: a private warning, a public warning, a temporary ban or a permanent ban, depending on how serious it is and whether it has happened before.
